Overview

Managing operational flow behind successful events.

As an Events Supervisor at Zereniti, I supported the planning and delivery of events ranging from smaller private gatherings to functions serving up to 600 guests.

My responsibility extended beyond supervising events on the day. I was involved in preparing the resources, labour, vendors and service requirements needed for an event to run smoothly, coordinating operations on the ground, monitoring guest numbers and food service, and completing post-event accountability.

The role required careful preparation, communication, prioritisation and the ability to respond to changes without disrupting the guest experience.

The Operational Challenge

High-stakes coordination across moving parts.

Every event had different requirements. Guest numbers varied, event formats changed, vendors had different delivery requirements, and some days involved more than one event. This meant that preparations had to be completed early enough to allow the team to respond if requirements changed before guests arrived.

My Approach

Prepare early, create room for change.

One of the most important principles I developed in the role was the value of early operational readiness. I aimed to have setups completed several hours before guests arrived whenever possible to create a buffer between preparation and guest arrival.

Food Service & Resource Control

Food service required close coordination with the kitchen and careful attention to guest numbers. I monitored the communicated guest headcount so that food preparation could be aligned with the expected number of guests.

At the same time, some additional food was planned for service readiness in case of small variations in attendance. I also monitored food wastage in coordination with the kitchen.

The objective was to balance two operational needs:

  • Service readiness — ensuring guests could be served properly.
  • Resource control — avoiding unnecessary surplus and monitoring wastage.

This gave me practical experience in balancing customer service requirements with operational resource considerations.

Managing Vendors and Event Resources

A significant part of the role involved coordinating physical resources required for an event, including crockery, plates, cutlery, food warmers, furniture, linens, flowers, vases, and setup equipment.

I communicated with vendors before and during events, checked that resources arrived as required, and ensured items were cleaned and ready before use. After events, I coordinated with vendors for the collection of their equipment. This required keeping track of what had arrived, what had been used and what needed to be returned.

Post-Event Accountability

My responsibility continued after guests had left. I supervised the cleaning and rearrangement of the event area and checked equipment and resources for issues such as breakages, burns, damage, or missing items.

I recorded these issues and presented the information to the accounts team the following day for accountability. I also coordinated vendor collection and participated in post-event meetings where the team reviewed what had gone well and identified areas for improvement.

This created a complete operational cycle:

Preparation → Execution → Monitoring → Recovery → Accountability → Review

Scale & Variety

The experience covered different event formats and capacities, including private gatherings, birthdays, picnics and larger functions. I supported events ranging from smaller gatherings of approximately 20–70 guests to functions serving up to 600 guests. The role could also involve coordinating different events within the same day, requiring careful prioritisation and scheduling.

What I Learned

The experience taught me that effective operations are not simply about responding quickly when something goes wrong. Good operations begin before the problem occurs.

By planning labour, confirming resources, preparing setups early and checking requirements in advance, I could create enough flexibility to respond when circumstances changed.
