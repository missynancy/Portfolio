Case study

Building a Repeatable Content System for SmartLukuFitness

Role: Content Operations & Technical VA
Type: Ongoing engagement
Skills shown: Content systems design, editorial process, technical content structuring, brand strategy, localization strategy
Live project: SmartLukuFitness Live Blog

The Challenge

SmartLukuFitness, a Kenyan lifestyle brand, needed a consistent content presence across Instagram and its blog — but had no repeatable system in place. Content was being created reactively, with no structure to guide what to post, when, or why. This made it difficult to stay consistent, track what was working, or hand the process off to anyone else in the future. The brand also needed its scope clarified: rather than fitness content alone, “Luku” called for a broader lifestyle identity spanning style, fitness, and nutrition.

What I Did

1. Defined the brand's three content pillars

Working with the brand name itself — “kupiga luku” is Kenyan slang for looking or dressing well — I helped shape SmartLukuFitness into a three-pillar lifestyle brand rather than a single-topic fitness page:

  • Style (“kupiga luku”) — looking and dressing well on a budget
  • Fitness — practical, no-gym-needed movement content
  • Nutrition — affordable, locally-grounded eating

2. Designed a recurring weekly content framework

I built a weekly content calendar structure with a defined pillar for each core content day, so content creation became a repeatable process rather than a blank-page problem every week:

  • Monday — Style / dressing well (“kupiga luku”)
  • Wednesday — Fitness
  • Friday — Nutrition
  • Remaining days — supporting content (quick tips, engagement, myth vs. fact, community/reflection)

This structure meant content could be planned in batches, weeks in advance, without decision fatigue on a daily basis, while keeping all three brand pillars consistently represented every week.

3. Localized the content strategy

Rather than using generic fitness advice, I grounded every piece of content in the local Kenyan context — referencing accessible ingredients (sukuma wiki, ndengu, omena, groundnuts), affordable local retailers (Naivas), and a “no gym needed, budget-conscious” positioning that matches the audience's real lifestyle and spending power. This is the kind of audience-first thinking that matters when building content for a specific market rather than a generic one.

4. Structured the technical publishing side

Beyond the content calendar, I also manage the brand's Astro-based blog, working directly with structured post data: title, slug, description, publish date, category, tags, read time, image and alt text, and featured/draft status. Each post includes a structured FAQ section, which supports both readability and on-page SEO.

The Outcome

  • A full week of content (Week 1) was planned, structured, and completed end-to-end using the new system, with Week 2 already underway.
  • Content creation shifted from ad hoc to a documented, repeatable weekly system — one that doesn't depend on starting from scratch, and could be handed off or scaled by someone else if needed.
  • The blog's technical structure (frontmatter fields, FAQ schema) was standardized, making future posts faster to produce and more consistent for SEO.

Why This Matters for Technical Ops Work

This project reflects the core of what I do as a Technical Ops VA: taking something unstructured — including the brand's own identity — and building a system around it that's repeatable and scalable. It wasn't just producing content; it was clarifying what the brand stood for, then designing the operational process to deliver on that consistently.
