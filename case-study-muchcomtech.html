From WhatsApp Status to Online Store: Building MuchcomTech

Client: Local electronics & household items retailer
Role: Web Developer, SEO Specialist, Project Manager
Live site: missynancy.github.io/MuchcomTech
Stack: React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS

The Challenge

The client sold electronics and household goods with no website. All sales happened through scrolling WhatsApp status updates, which made it hard for customers to browse products, compare prices, or find what they wanted quickly. There was no searchable catalog and no professional online presence.

The Solution

I designed and built a full e-commerce storefront from the ground up:

  • Product catalog — browsable by category (smartphones, laptops, audio, wearables, gaming, cameras), with detailed product pages, specs, pricing, and image galleries. Additional categories, including household items, are built into the codebase and ready to switch on as the client expands inventory.
  • Direct WhatsApp checkout — customers can add items to a cart and complete their order straight through WhatsApp, with a form-to-email option built in as an alternative.
  • Fully responsive design — optimized for mobile, where most customers browse and shop.
  • Modern, maintainable stack — built with React, TypeScript, and Vite for speed and long-term maintainability.
  • SEO-ready — structured metadata, Open Graph tags for clean social/WhatsApp link previews, and search-engine-friendly markup.

Performance Results

After deployment, I ran the site through Google PageSpeed Insights and iterated on real, measurable improvements:

Metric Before After
Performance 92 94
Accessibility 83 94
Best Practices 100 100
SEO 100 100

Key optimizations:

  • Converted product images to WebP and right-sized them for their actual display dimensions, cutting image payload by an estimated 260+ KB
  • Added aria-labels to icon-only navigation elements (cart, search) for screen reader accessibility
  • Fixed low-contrast text for better readability
  • Resolved React Router configuration for correct sub-path routing on GitHub Pages

The Business Arrangement

To keep this a low-risk first step for the client, I built and deliver the site itself at no development cost — the client covers domain and hosting once they're ready to move off the free GitHub Pages URL. I'm also managing and updating the site directly for the first two months.

Outcome

The client now has a live, fast, mobile-friendly online store with direct WhatsApp ordering — replacing an informal, hard-to-browse status-update sales process with a proper searchable catalog and checkout flow.
