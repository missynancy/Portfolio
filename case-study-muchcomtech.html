Case study

From WhatsApp Status to Online Store: Building MuchcomTech

Client: Local electronics & household items business
Role: Web Developer, SEO Specialist, Project Manager
Live site: missynancy.github.io/MuchcomTech
Stack: React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS

The Challenge

The client sold electronics — everything from digital gadgets to household items — with no website. All sales happened through scrolling WhatsApp status updates, which made it hard for customers to browse products, compare prices, or find what they wanted quickly. There was no way to showcase the full catalog, no searchable product listing, and no professional online presence.

The Solution

I designed and built a full e-commerce storefront from the ground up:

  • Product catalog — browsable by category (smartphones, laptops, audio, wearables, gaming, cameras), with detailed product pages, specs, pricing, and image galleries
  • Direct WhatsApp checkout — customers can add items to cart and complete their order straight through WhatsApp, with a form-to-email option built in as an alternative
  • Fully responsive design — optimized for mobile, where most customers browse and shop
  • Fast, modern tech stack — built with React, TypeScript, and Vite for a fast, maintainable codebase
  • SEO-ready — structured metadata, Open Graph tags for clean social/WhatsApp link previews, and search-engine-friendly markup

Performance Results

After deployment, I ran the site through Google PageSpeed Insights and iterated on real, measurable improvements:

Metric Before After
Performance 92 94
Accessibility 83 94
Best Practices 100 100
SEO 100 100
Agentic Browsing 1/2 2/2

Key optimizations:

  • Converted product images to WebP format and right-sized them for their actual display dimensions, cutting image payload from 313 KB down to under 50 KB of estimated savings
  • Added aria-labels to icon-only navigation elements (cart, search) for screen reader accessibility
  • Fixed low-contrast text for better readability
  • Resolved React Router configuration for correct sub-path routing on GitHub Pages

The Business Arrangement

To keep this a low-risk first step for the client, I offered the site itself for free — the client only covers the domain cost — and I'm managing and updating the site at no charge for the first two months. This let me deliver a real, live project for my portfolio while giving the client a working, professional storefront with zero upfront cost.

Outcome

The client now has a live, fast, mobile-friendly online store with direct WhatsApp ordering — replacing an informal, hard-to-browse status-update sales process with a proper searchable catalog and checkout flow.
